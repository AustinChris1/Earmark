# What people use it for

Earmark does one thing: a group puts money together, and it can only reach the place that was named when the collection opened. That one rule fits a lot of situations where someone currently has to be trusted to pass money on.

Two kinds of collection, and it is worth knowing which you need:

- **To a wallet.** The money goes straight to a locked address, in the same transaction as the payment. Earmark never holds it.
- **To a bill.** Some things have no wallet: a phone number, a prepaid meter. The group collects into Earmark's wallet, Earmark pays the provider, and anything unused comes back. This is the only case where Earmark holds money, and only until the bill is paid.

---

## 1. Rent for a parent in another country

Four siblings in three countries pay their mother's rent. Today one of them collects everything and sends it on, and everyone else takes it on trust.

Open a collection in the landlord's currency, locked to the landlord's wallet. Each sibling pays in whatever they hold, naira, reais or dollars, and Earmark swaps it on the way, so the landlord only ever receives the currency the rent is set in. Nobody has to be the treasurer.

## 2. School fees

A family pays a term's fees for a child. The fear is not theft, it is that the money arrives and gets spent on something urgent before it reaches the school.

Lock the collection to the school's wallet. Everyone can see the total, who has paid, and that the destination cannot be changed, including by whoever opened it.

## 3. The light bill at home

Nobody wants to be the one who tops up the meter every month and chases people afterwards.

Open a bill collection for the meter number. When it fills, Earmark buys the units itself and posts the token in the group. Unused money goes back to the people who paid.

## 4. Airtime for a parent

The smallest and most common one. A top-up for a phone, split between siblings, with no argument about who sent what.

A bill collection for the phone number. It works for a Nigerian line, and for a number in many other countries.

## 5. A group trip

Six friends book flights. One person pays the airline and spends weeks collecting from everyone else.

Lock the collection to the wallet of whoever is booking, split it evenly, and let the bot do the chasing. Everyone gets their own link, and the tally says who is still out.

## 6. A savings circle

In a rotating savings group, every month one member receives everyone's contribution. The whole thing runs on trust in whoever is holding the pot.

Each month, open a collection locked to that month's recipient. The destination is fixed before anyone pays, and the record of who paid is in the chat and on chain.

## 7. Association dues

A residents' association, an alumni group or a church collects a service charge from many people. The treasurer ends up reconciling bank transfers by hand.

One collection, one locked destination, and a tally that updates itself. Instalments spread it over weeks for people who cannot pay at once.

## 8. A hospital bill

The most painful version of the same problem, usually raised quickly between relatives who do not all know each other.

Lock the collection to the hospital's wallet. People who have never met the organiser can still see where the money can go before they send any.

## 9. A relative abroad who will not install anything

Someone wants to help but has no local bank account and no interest in a new app.

They open the link, pay from any Celo wallet in whatever they hold, and that is the whole interaction. No Telegram, no account, no app.

## 10. An agent paying on someone's behalf

A person's own AI agent settles their share, with no wallet screen and no app.

Every open collection is an x402 endpoint, so an agent pays with one command and the payment is counted in the group tally like any other.

---

## When Earmark is the wrong tool

- **Paying a person you simply trust.** Send them the money.
- **Anything needing the destination changed later.** It cannot be changed, for anyone, for any reason, including a mistyped address. That is the product.
- **A bill Earmark cannot pay and that has no wallet,** for example a water board that only takes bank transfers. The nearest thing is a collection locked to the wallet of whoever will pay it locally, who can then cash out to their bank.
